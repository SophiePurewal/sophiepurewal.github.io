Challenge

Creating consistency across complex product journeys

Multiple product teams needed accessible, reusable UI patterns that could support complex digital services without repeatedly solving the same interface problems. Fragmented legacy patterns and evolving requirements created inconsistency across forms, navigation, feedback and layout.

My work focused on modernising shared components, defining their behaviour and states, progressing them through design and engineering governance, and measuring reuse across files and teams.

Operating at scale

Contributing inside a heavily adopted design system

The Core library I worked within contained 461 components, 454 variables and 143 shared styles, and was used by 17 teams at the time the analytics were captured. In the latest measured week alone, the library recorded 18.4k component inserts.

Annual analytics also showed sustained usage across the year, including weekly peaks above 20,000 inserts. These are library-level adoption metrics rather than a claim that I personally created the entire system; they provide the organisational context for the components I designed and modernised.

Outcome

A measurable contribution within a system operating at scale

Within a Core library used by 17 teams, the components I built and modernised recorded more than 21,000 Figma-tracked insertions across header, status, feedback, layout and form patterns over the measured 12-month period.

The wider library context — 461 components, 454 variables, 143 styles and 18.4k inserts in the latest measured week — shows the scale of the ecosystem in which this work operated. My component-level adoption data demonstrates reuse across files and teams, supported by governance, documentation, engineering collaboration and adoption measurement.

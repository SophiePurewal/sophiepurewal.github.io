Financial services case study

Rebranding a regulated financial service

I redesigned components for Aegon’s new brand website and played a central role in UI quality assurance, manually reviewing the responsive experience page by page to ensure the brand, components and underlying structure were implemented consistently before launch.

Role
Senior UI Designer
Contribution
Component redesign and UI UAT
Platform
Responsive financial-services website

Overview

Translating a new brand into a dependable digital experience

The service needed to explain a complex employee benefit in plain language and guide employers and employees towards relevant next steps. At the same time, the website was being brought into Aegon’s new visual identity across a large set of responsive pages and reusable components.

My responsibility was to turn the new brand direction into consistent, production-ready UI and help protect that quality through launch. I redesigned components, reviewed their use across the website and identified visual, structural and interaction issues through detailed UAT.

My role

Owning component redesign and UI quality assurance

  • Redesigned components for the new visual identity
  • Updated the salary-sacrifice calculator and interaction states
  • Reviewed responsive layouts, hierarchy and component structure
  • Led detailed UI checks across the website during UAT
  • Documented issues and worked with engineering and product through resolution

Design approach

Applying the new brand without compromising usability

The work required more than a visual reskin. Each redesigned component needed to express the new brand while remaining clear, accessible, responsive and structurally sound. I balanced brand fidelity with established interaction patterns and prioritised issues according to their effect on comprehension, consistency and successful task completion.

Working within constraints

Improving the interface without destabilising established journeys or delivery commitments.

Prioritising user impact

Focusing attention on clarity, interaction states, responsive behaviour and areas involving complex financial information.

Collaborating through delivery

Working with product owners, engineers and project managers through the Scrum process to reach practical resolutions.

Before the redesign

The previous website experience

These desktop and mobile examples show the website before the new brand was applied. They provided an important reference point for assessing how hierarchy, styling and reusable components needed to evolve across the responsive experience.

After the rebrand

The redesigned responsive experience

The website experience needed to communicate the broader service proposition clearly across devices. I reviewed how hierarchy, content, navigation and calls to action translated from desktop to mobile so the experience remained clear, readable and easy to act on.

  • Simple page hierarchy that supported scanning and comparison
  • Plain-language content and guidance for a complex financial proposition
  • Responsive layout patterns that kept key content readable on smaller screens
  • Calls to action that supported exploration without forcing commitment too early

Component redesign

Redesigning the salary-sacrifice calculator

The calculator enabled users to enter salary information and see potential savings generated through salary sacrifice. The experience needed to make a financial calculation feel simple and accessible while maintaining clarity and confidence.

I redesigned the calculator interface to align it with the new identity, brand standards and established component patterns. This included the presentation of guidance, inputs, validation and calculated outcomes across desktop and smaller screens.

  • Alignment with Aegon’s identity and visual brand standards
  • Consistent typography, spacing and component styling
  • Clear visual hierarchy between guidance, inputs and outcomes
  • Consistent validation and interaction states
  • Responsive behaviour on smaller screens
  • Alignment with established design-system patterns

Agile delivery

Maintaining design quality through collaborative UAT

I was instrumental in the UI side of UAT. Together with the Agile delivery team, I worked through the website manually, page by page and across screen sizes, to confirm that the new brand had been translated correctly and that the components, page structure and interactions were sound.

This detailed review connected design intent with the live implementation. I identified issues affecting visual consistency, responsive behaviour, component usage, hierarchy and usability, documented them with clear context and worked with product owners, engineers and project managers to reach practical resolutions.

  1. Review the implemented page or component within the delivery cycle
  2. Compare it with the approved design, Aegon brand standards and established UI patterns
  3. Record the issue clearly with supporting context
  4. Prioritise issues with the team based on user impact and severity
  5. Discuss practical fixes with product owners, engineers and project managers through the Scrum workflow
  6. Recheck the implementation after changes were made

This process made UI quality assurance a visible part of delivery rather than a final cosmetic check. It helped the team resolve inconsistencies systematically and gave the launch experience a stronger, more dependable visual and structural foundation.

Outcome

Supporting a consistent, brand-aligned release

My contribution helped translate Aegon’s new brand into a consistent responsive website through redesigned components, a brand-aligned salary-sacrifice calculator and rigorous UI UAT across the wider experience.

The project demonstrates my ability to combine visual design craft with systematic quality assurance: defining how a brand should work at component level, reviewing how it behaves in production and collaborating closely with engineering and product colleagues to resolve issues before launch.

Reflection

What this delivery phase reinforced

  • Brand transformation needs to be tested at component, page and journey level
  • UAT is most effective when it is embedded within the Agile delivery process
  • Small component decisions can significantly affect consistency
  • Clear issue documentation improves collaboration with product and engineering
  • Complex financial interfaces need clear hierarchy, plain-language guidance and well-signposted next steps

Commercial product and UI design case study

Financial services platform and salary-sacrifice calculator

Making a complex employee benefit easier to understand through responsive interface refinement, calculator UI and UAT.

The project centred on presenting a financial-services proposition in a clearer, more approachable way for employers and employees. The experience needed to explain the value of the wider service, support confident decision-making and guide users towards a practical calculator flow.

I joined during the later delivery phase, when the website’s core structure and journeys were already established. My contribution focused on selected component improvements, parts of the salary-sacrifice calculator, responsive design review and UAT ahead of release.

Context and challenge

The wider service needed to make a complex employee benefit easier to understand, explain the proposition in plain language and guide employers and employees towards relevant next steps.

The project was already well advanced when I joined, so my role was not to redefine the entire product. I needed to understand the existing patterns quickly, identify inconsistencies and usability risks, and make focused improvements within the established design system, delivery plan and technical implementation.

This meant balancing reassurance and clarity with practical routes to action, without disrupting established journeys or delivery commitments.

My contribution

  • UI refinement of selected production components
  • Redesign of parts of the salary-sacrifice calculator
  • Responsive layout and component review
  • Visual consistency against established design-system patterns
  • Review of interaction states and error handling
  • Identification and documentation of UAT issues
  • Collaboration with product, engineering and delivery colleagues
  • Verification of fixes before release

Responsive website experience

Beyond the calculator, I reviewed selected areas of the broader website experience across desktop and mobile. The service needed to explain its proposition clearly, answer common questions and guide users towards relevant next steps without overwhelming them.

My review focused on whether the interface maintained a clear hierarchy, readable content and consistent routes to action across screen sizes.

  • Simple page hierarchy that supported scanning and comparison
  • Plain-language content and guidance for a complex financial proposition
  • Responsive layout patterns that kept key information readable on smaller screens
  • Calls to action that supported exploration without forcing commitment too early

Salary-sacrifice calculator

Helping users understand potential savings through a focused calculator flow was one of the main areas I contributed to.

The calculator needed to make a complex employee benefit easier to understand while presenting financial inputs, supporting guidance and calculated outcomes in a logical sequence. I refined selected parts of the interface to improve hierarchy, reduce friction and align the experience with the wider design system.

  • Clearer visual hierarchy between guidance, inputs and outcomes
  • More consistent spacing and alignment
  • Improved grouping of related inputs
  • Clearer distinction between editable fields and calculated outputs
  • Review of responsive behaviour on smaller screens
  • Review of validation and interaction states
  • Alignment with existing component patterns

UAT and design-quality assurance

A significant part of my contribution involved reviewing the implemented website during UAT. I assessed the interface across pages and screen sizes, comparing the live implementation with the intended design and identifying issues that could affect clarity, consistency or usability.

  1. Review the implemented page or component
  2. Compare it with the approved design and established UI patterns
  3. Record the issue clearly with supporting context
  4. Prioritise issues based on user impact and severity
  5. Discuss fixes with the relevant delivery colleagues
  6. Recheck the implementation after changes were made

This work helped protect final design quality during delivery, without implying that every defect across the website was found or resolved.

Senior product and UI design judgement

Joining late in delivery required a different kind of design judgement from leading an early-stage concept. I needed to understand the existing system quickly, distinguish between critical issues and lower-priority polish, and recommend improvements that were realistic within the remaining delivery constraints.

Working within constraints

Improving the interface without destabilising established journeys or delivery commitments.

Prioritising user impact

Focusing attention on clarity, interaction states, responsive behaviour and areas involving complex financial information.

Collaborating through delivery

Communicating issues clearly and working with product and engineering colleagues to reach practical resolutions.

Outcome

My contribution supported the final stages of delivery by improving selected production components, strengthening the route into and through the salary-sacrifice calculator, and helping the team identify and resolve UI issues during UAT.

The project demonstrates my ability to contribute effectively within an established product environment, work carefully with regulated financial content and maintain design quality during a pressured delivery phase.

What I learned

  • Late-stage design work requires strong prioritisation
  • UAT is an important part of product-design quality
  • Small component decisions can significantly affect consistency
  • Clear issue documentation improves collaboration with engineering
  • Complex financial interfaces need clear hierarchy, plain-language guidance and well-signposted next steps
